# Feature research and the evidence connection: the plan

Researched Nov 6–7, 2026 across five sweeps (running apps, strength and recovery apps, dedicated rehab apps, literature APIs, evidence-in-apps patterns), each followed by a verification pass against primary sources. Nothing here is built. Nothing here changes a prescription: program.md owns training content, and every candidate below is information, ergonomics, or documentation unless Shawn writes a rule.

A network caveat applies throughout: several vendor hosts (examine.com, whoop.com, curovate.com, exakthealth.com, runna.com help pages, support.google.com) were unreachable from the research session, so some claims rest on app-store listings, press releases, and search excerpts of official pages. Every claim verification could not confirm at a primary source is marked **(unverified)** or **(secondary source only)**. The literature APIs in section 3 were tested live — CORS headers, rate-limit headers, and real queries — so those claims are first-hand.

## 1. What the common apps have that this app lacks

The field splits cleanly: charts that inform (Strava Fitness & Freshness, TrainingPeaks PMC, intervals.icu) and engines that prescribe (Garmin Daily Suggested Workouts, Runna adaptive paces, Fitbod, Peloton IQ). knee-rebuild already has the prescribing half — pain-driven, which none of them are — so almost everything worth importing is from the informing half. Notably, no surveyed consumer rehab app auto-gates on objective symmetry tests the way the phase gates do; the pain rules + test-gated phases + user-confirmed advance combination is ahead of the commercial field. The validated outcome questionnaires (KOOS, ACL-RSI) live in the clinical-trial layer, not in any shipping app — which makes them cheap differentiators here.

## 2. Recommended candidates, in priority order

This is a rehab app, so the rehab-specific instruments outrank the fitness-app conveniences.

1. **ACL-RSI behind the gate-3 trust toggle.** Seed the 12-item ACL-RSI as a form reachable from the Gates screen; store dated scores the way hop tests are stored; show the latest score in words next to the manual "would trust it at full speed" item. The score never passes or fails the item — Shawn's toggle does — but the number gives the toggle a memory. Smallest build with the most rehab substance: one form, one table row shape, one line on Gates.

2. **Periodic KOOS, with Tegner as a companion.** A KOOS entry offered monthly and at each gate attempt, scored in `src/rules/` (the scoring arithmetic is published and fixed), trended on Progress with the five subscales as words and numbers. It is the instrument the ACL-app trials use as their primary outcome and no consumer app ships; it answers "is this working" with something validated rather than vibes. Cadence and placement are Shawn's call (section 4).

3. **Evidence references on the Program screen.** The Exakt Health pattern at plan granularity, done the knee-rebuild way: a hand-curated `data/evidence.json` (citation, DOI, link, study type, one-sentence takeaway, an evidence-grade word, a hand-entered PEDro score where one exists with the required PEDro Partnership acknowledgment), Zod-validated, rendered read-only per phase and exercise pattern. Evidence is presentation, never decision: no citation changes a prescription. Section 3 covers where the entries come from.

4. **Fitness/fatigue/form chart plus the EWMA upgrade.** One new pure function: exponentially weighted CTL (42-day), ATL (7-day) and their difference over the sRPE loads already logged, drawn on Progress with worded states, TRIMP joining when phase 2 lands. In the same change, move the acute:chronic warning from rolling windows to EWMA, keeping the worded thresholds, with a PROGRAM-QUESTIONS note that the numbers will shift slightly at the seam.

5. **Planned-vs-actual compliance in words, plus the adherence count.** The new part is sets-based lift compliance — "all 9 prescribed sets", "7 of 9 sets", "stopped early" on the session summary (TrainingPeaks' own pattern for strength) — and a completed-vs-scheduled count per week beside the consecutive-clean-days line (no streak mechanics: skipping on a bad knee day is correct behavior, and swaps and deloads count as adherent). Weekly miles against target already exists on Week, the run logger and Progress, so that part is done. Words and shapes, no color-only meaning, feeds nothing.

6. **Session-runner ergonomics: plate math, ghost values, machine notes.** Three small things that pay rent every session: the per-side plate breakdown under the load stepper for `smith-plates` slots, last session's load and reps shown faint beside today's prefill, and a persistent setup note per exercise per gym next to the cues. All display-only; the machine note is the only schema touch.

7. **Morning-check tags with local impact trends.** Five or six fixed tags on the morning check (poor sleep, long sitting, brace worn, NSAID taken, new shoes), correlated on-device against next-day readings once there's enough data, reported in plain counts: "after poor sleep: 3 of 5 readings were hold". No AI, no composite score, nothing leaves the phone. One honest caveat: PROMPT section 8 currently rules out tracking beyond the knee and the training, so this one starts with Shawn amending that non-goal (section 4, decision 10) or it doesn't start.

8. **The 4-hop gate battery with a quality note.** Extend the gate-test item list to the standard four hops, keep three-trials-best-kept, add an optional free-text "how it looked" per test. Earns its place because gate 2 and 3 symmetry is the spine of the program and one hop is a thin sample of it.

Explicitly not recommended, so the decisions stay on record: composite readiness scores, a second prescription engine of any kind, AI summaries, HR-stream effort scores, camera form feedback, phone ROM measurement, segments and streak mechanics.

## 3. The peer-reviewed connection

Three shapes were compared. All three respect the same floor: the page talks only to its own origin, on tap; nothing from Strava ever reaches an AI feature; no citation changes a prescription.

**(a) Curated citation layer, built at build time — recommended.** A script in `scripts/` (sibling to `scripts/program.ts`) queries Europe PMC and PubMed during development; Shawn and Claude curate the results into `data/evidence.json`; the app renders it read-only. The phone makes zero literature calls, ever. This is candidate 3 above and should be built first.

**(b) On-tap search, `api/evidence.ts` — optional, later.** A third Vercel function proxying Europe PMC (no key, so no secret to hold), same public-route protection the Strava and photo functions need, results shown transiently, only a saved citation kept. Even though Europe PMC sends open CORS headers and the browser could technically call it directly, the app's own rule — no runtime external calls except its own /api functions on tap — routes it through the function. Additive; nothing in (a) depends on it.

**(c) Monthly digest.** Not an in-app feature at all: a periodic Claude Code session that re-runs the (a) queries, diffs against `evidence.json`, and proposes updates as a PR Shawn reviews. This keeps the page fully static, puts a human on every change, and is the natural mitigation for stale citations. Recommended as the maintenance mode of (a), not as a separate build.

### Recommended architecture

`scripts/evidence.ts` (dev-time only) hits Europe PMC `resultType=core` and PubMed esearch/efetch; output is reviewed by hand into `data/evidence.json`, validated by a Zod schema in `src/schema/`; each entry carries citation, DOI, links, study type, a worded evidence grade from a small fixed vocabulary (the GRADE words — high / moderate / low / very low — are words, not colors, which suits the colorblind rule), a one-sentence takeaway, optional hand-entered PEDro score with acknowledgment. The Program screen renders entries beside the phase and pattern they speak to. The phone stays offline-capable; the bundle holds no keys because none are needed. If (b) is ever wanted, `api/evidence.ts` proxies Europe PMC with the same route protection as `api/strava.ts` and `api/photo.ts`.

### Example queries, validated live on PubMed (Nov 6, 2026)

- `"Anterior Cruciate Ligament Injuries/rehabilitation"[MeSH]` → 526 records; adding `AND (systematic[sb] OR randomized controlled trial[pt]) AND 2020/01/01:3000[dp]` → 92 — a hand-curatable set.
- Because the newest records lack MeSH indexing, pair it with a text-word block: `("anterior cruciate ligament"[tiab] OR ACL[tiab]) AND (rehabilit*[tiab] OR "return to sport"[tiab] OR exercise[tiab])`.
- Also useful: `"Return to Sport"[Mesh]`, `"Anterior Cruciate Ligament Reconstruction"[Mesh]`, and — closer to Shawn's actual injury — `"Knee Injuries/rehabilitation"[Mesh]`, since most of the literature is post-surgical ACLR and his sprain is non-operative and multi-ligament.
- Europe PMC equivalents: the keyword query plus `JOURNAL:"Cochrane database syst rev"` for reviews (48 live hits), `PUB_TYPE:"Randomized Controlled Trial"`, `OPEN_ACCESS:y`.
- The search appendices of Cochrane reviews CD010128 and CD005961 are good templates for a sensitivity-maximizing RCT block.

### Barriers, each with its mitigation

- **Licensing and copyright.** Metadata and citations are free to use; abstracts may carry publisher copyright and full text usually does. Mitigation: `evidence.json` stores citation, link, and a takeaway sentence written by Shawn/Claude — never pasted abstract or review text; any cached full text gated on the per-article license field Europe PMC returns.
- **Abstracts vs full text.** Most of what the APIs return is abstract-deep, and abstracts overstate. Mitigation: the takeaway sentence is written after reading the actual paper (OA) or marked "abstract only" when it wasn't; the evidence-grade word carries the uncertainty.
- **Rate limits and API churn.** OpenAlex removed its free polite pool with ~3 months' notice in Feb 2026; Crossref changed limits in Dec 2025. Mitigation: the build-time design makes churn a dev-time annoyance, not an app outage; Europe PMC and PubMed, the two the plan depends on, are publicly funded infrastructure with the stablest terms.
- **The medical-device line.** FDA's general-wellness guidance (reissued Jan 2026) and EU MDR Rule 11 both attach to an intended purpose for a product placed on a market. A single-user tool Shawn built for himself is not placed on any market, so citing evidence creates no exposure — the personal-use reading of MDR scope is inference, **not verified against the regulation text**. The line becomes real only if the app is ever shared: a distributed app citing evidence to drive rehab decisions for a knee injury is a condition-treatment claim under both regimes, not general wellness. That sentence is the reason this paragraph exists: a future "open-source it" impulse should trip over it.
- **Cherry-picking, staleness, overclaiming.** The documented industry anti-patterns (a 2024 audit reported under 44% of health apps stating any evidence, over 70% of those on low-quality studies — **secondary source only**). Mitigations: the search strategy is saved in the script so the result set is reproducible, not hand-picked; the monthly digest (c) re-runs it and diffs; each entry's grade word and study type stop a mechanism paper from dressing up as proof the app's rule works; and `evidence.json` cites evidence *about* the rules, it never generates or modifies a rule.
- **PEDro's terms.** No automation, period. Mitigation: PEDro scores are typed in by hand for the handful of trials that warrant them, with the acknowledgment line rendered.
- **The Strava-AI wall is untouched.** The evidence layer involves no AI at runtime and never sees activity data; the curation sessions read papers, not Shawn's logs.

## 4. What needs Shawn's decision before any build

1. **KOOS cadence and placement.** Monthly, per gate attempt, or both — and whether the score appears on the Gates screen at all, or only on Progress, so a dipping subscale never reads as an extra gate item.
2. **ACL-RSI next to the trust toggle.** Confirm the score informs the toggle and never evaluates it.
3. **Warm-up ramps.** Training content program.md doesn't currently specify. Goes to PROGRAM-QUESTIONS with a proposed default; built only on sign-off.
4. **The planned down-period.** The one candidate that changes prescriptions. Proposed conservative default: every day of a declared 3–14 day period counts as a hold reading. program.md decision.
5. **EWMA swap on the warning.** Same thresholds, slightly different numbers at the seam; Shawn should say yes knowing the 1.5× line will fire at marginally different moments than the rolling-window version did.
6. **Forward load-budget wording** — or whether to build it at all, given how easily "room left" reads as a target.
7. **Benchmark bands.** Which, if any, published numbers enter program.md as context lines, given they come from post-surgical ACLR cohorts and his injury is non-operative.
8. **Evidence layer scope.** The grade-word vocabulary (proposed: the four GRADE words), per-phase vs per-pattern granularity, whether gate rationales get citations, and whether the on-tap search function (b) is wanted at all for v1 of this work (proposed: no — build (a) with the digest (c) as its maintenance mode).
9. **Adherence framing.** Confirm the completed-vs-scheduled count (folded into candidate 5) carries no streak mechanics and that swaps and deload weeks count as adherent.
10. **Morning-check tags vs the non-goal.** Candidate 7 tracks sleep and life factors, which PROMPT section 8 currently excludes. Amend the non-goal for these five or six fixed tags, or drop the candidate.
11. **The 4-hop battery's standing.** Whether the three added hops are informational records beside the existing gate items or become required items — gate criteria are training content, so making them required is a program.md change.
