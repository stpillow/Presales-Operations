## Notable trap-check evidence

- **deal-strategy** rated the never-engaged economic buyer ❌ (not ⚠️), marked unobservable execution elements ➖ ("No SA assigned; there is no solution design to assess"), enforced blackout ("the champion cannot be worked directly"), and asked who shaped the RFP requirements.

- **proposal-manager** requested the full solicitation instead of shredding from a summary ("TBD (cite on receipt)"), fenced the unverifiable requirement ("Do not write 'we integrate with Tyler' anywhere until an SME confirms"), kept bid/no-bid a recommendation, and scheduled submission 24+ hours early.
- **enablement-planner** challenged the training framing ("11 minutes isn't discovery — it's a scheduling call"), named the mixed skill/content/process gap with the confound, priced capacity at ~8-10% before RFP season, and used behavioral anchors ("recovers from hiccups without losing the room").
- **career-development** drafted the missing ladder before the IDP, marked two competencies "insufficient evidence" rather than rating on impressions, built a 70/20/10 plan, declined comp guidance, and deferred the promotion decision to HR/calibration.

## Caveats for reading a 35/35 baseline

1. The checks were derived from the same SKILL.md guardrails the runners followed — a green baseline mostly proves internal consistency, not real-world fitness. The suite's real value starts now: it catches **regressions** as we tailor.
2. Single-judge verdicts; borderline checks (behavioral-anchor quality, "every recommendation tied to a metric") can flip between runs. Treat isolated future failures on those checks as possible judge noise before editing skills.
3. Runner sessions approximate but don't exactly reproduce the production environment (no personal CLAUDE.md, no MCP tools). Occasionally run a scenario for real as calibration.

**Cost:** ~10 agents, ~255K subagent tokens for the full suite.
